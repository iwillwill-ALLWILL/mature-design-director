# Presentation and Competition Capability

Use this capability for premium decks, competition submissions, investor/product pitches, demo-day materials, launch presentations, and evidence-heavy reports. For `.pptx`, also load presentation mechanics; for PDF/HTML delivery use the applicable format adapter. This capability owns narrative, judging strategy, visual direction, evidence composition, and submission-safe QA—not low-level file-format mechanics already covered elsewhere.

Enter full-deck production only from the umbrella's `selected-direction`. Approved page boards, an existing brand/editorial system, a licensed template visibly adopted for its language, and real evidence media can form the creative foundation. PowerPoint, Slidev, PptxGenJS, PDF conversion, and layout code are assembly mechanics.

## Truth and artifact strategy

Before design, inspect the authoritative rules or delivery contract and record:

- required, optional, linked, prohibited, and finalist-only artifacts;
- file formats, size limits, naming/ZIP rules, deadline and timezone;
- portal fields, links, resubmission behavior, and network/offline constraints;
- judging weights, tie-break signals, and what prior winners/finalists actually used when official evidence exists;
- planned, simulated, local, deployed, and production truth boundaries;
- current state: registered, preview ready, awaiting approval, submitted, awarded.

When a portal is dynamic, contradictory, or partially login-gated, use current first-party rules/API data, then authorized portal inspection; label unresolved ambiguity rather than treating a third-party summary as authority.

For judged work, compare at least:

1. compliance-minimum package;
2. balanced evidence package;
3. win-max package.

Score marginal rubric coverage, judge attention cost, portal/format risk, link fragility, and fallback behavior. The official minimum is a floor, not automatically the best package; more files are also not automatically better. Every supplement must earn its place.

Keep the official upload directory limited to the exact permitted set. Store editable source, QA renders, optional supplements, manifests, and receipts outside it unless the rules require a combined archive.

## Representative-direction gate

Recover the strongest prior approved deck/source before fresh ideation. Build only three intended-scale representative page boards first:

- cover/emotional thesis;
- densest product/evidence page;
- proof, architecture, or closing page.

Show their static resting frames and motion behavior when motion is material. Compare them with named mature deck references and do not produce the full deck until they pass the umbrella selection gate. When the user asked to score examples, show these page boards first. A rejected direction becomes non-final and isolated; do not continue polishing it under another filename.

Prefer one dominant image or idea per page, asymmetric editorial pacing, concise type, deliberate density variation, and a small number of meaningful palette accents. Avoid repeated rounded cards, equal three-column layouts, tiny full-page screenshots, decorative KPIs, and generic competition labels.

## Narrative and source map

Assign every claim to the strongest honest visual source:

| Claim class | Preferred source |
|---|---|
| Human context/future vision | licensed or generated atmosphere, visibly non-evidentiary |
| Product behavior | real screenshot or recording |
| Consent/security/privacy | real control or policy crop |
| Execution/recovery | real before/action/after/audit sequence |
| Engineering quality | reproducible command output with denominator, date, scope |
| Architecture | concrete example flow; abstraction only when it clarifies |
| Roadmap | explicitly planned/proposed material |

Alternate scene, evidence, typography, diagram, and measured proof so the deck does not become a screenshot dump or generated-image monoculture.

## Generated-scene boundary

Generated scenes establish atmosphere, not product proof. Reject pseudo-writing, fake screens, malformed hands, duplicated hardware, sterile CGI passed as deployment, and unlabeled concept imagery adjacent to evidence.

Safer patterns include powered-off displays, face-down phones, architecture/interior scenes with natural imperfection, and real product crops beside the concept scene. Label ambiguity concisely, but regenerate visibly bad imagery rather than relying on a disclaimer.

## Evidence crops and semantic grounding

Capture complete proof regions rather than dropping unreadable full pages. Keep source, state, control, outcome, and audit context necessary to support the claim. Use `contain` by default for evidence.

Validate the **final rendered page at ordinary viewing scale**:

- OCR judge-critical phrases as a regression guard;
- make lossless tight crops from the real artifact when necessary;
- record source/crop coordinates in internal provenance;
- never recreate product text editorially and call it evidence;
- re-export and inspect after every crop change.

Preserve semantic operators: before/after, no later than, at most, ranges, uncertainty, and simulated/real status. If a deck exposes a product/schema bug, fix the product and recapture instead of rewriting the slide around it.

For metrics, include denominator, exact command/workflow, date/timezone, scope, workspace status, and bounded error claim. Prefer `95/95 in this dated suite` over unsupported giant numbers.

For a minimal reproducible evidence snapshot, rebuild from a clean environment with only the permitted source, dependencies, commands, and truth-boundary labels; do not reuse source caches, credentials, browser state, or unrelated repository history.

## Assembly and delivery modes

Choose deliberately:

1. populate a supplied or licensed template;
2. compose authored HTML/CSS pages and export;
3. render complete pages and package one image per slide when fidelity outranks editability;
4. use native editable slide elements when editability is required.

Disclose image-backed/non-editable tradeoffs. Programmatic authoring is assembly mechanics, not visual authorship. Do not let PptxGenJS or another programmatic tool invent or approve the visual grammar from repeated rectangles. Preserve a strong approved motion source in its native/high-fidelity channel rather than replacing it with stiff motion merely to fit PPTX.

Use motion or offline dynamic delivery only when it genuinely increases judging/user value. Preserve the QA-passed static fallback, verify playback in the exact target viewer, and do not substitute a palette-limited full-slide GIF for native timing or a high-quality video fallback.

## Reverse-render and package QA

Required loop:

1. render every page;
2. create a contact sheet;
3. inspect full-size risk pages;
4. run independent visual/content review;
5. fix and re-render;
6. package PPTX/PDF;
7. reverse-render the exported binary;
8. verify page count, aspect ratio, clipping, crop, fonts, image quality, links, metadata, OOXML/ZIP integrity, and hashes;
9. compare final source/artifact freshness;
10. reproduce the packaging chain in a clean environment when submission risk is high.

A changed source with an unchanged final binary is stale, regardless of a tool's success message. A tall contact sheet that the user cannot see is not delivered; provide representative/full pages separately.

A visible-clean deck can still contain dormant template text, stale metadata, external relationships, or hidden parts. Under literal no-placeholder requirements, package residue is a failure or explicit risk.

## Approval and submission

Track separately: generated, technically checked, visually reviewed, visibly delivered, exact binary approved, submitted, receipt verified.

Approval is bound to the exact artifact/hash and reviewed dimension. Video approval is not deck approval; deck approval is not upload authorization. Any binary-changing edit invalidates approval.

At login/upload handoff, state only:

1. what is finished and whether it is submitted;
2. what the agent will do next;
3. the user's one required login/OTP action;
4. the resume signal.

After submission, record platform ID, timestamp/timezone, receipt, uploaded hash, and final state. Never persist live QR tickets in skills or receipts.
