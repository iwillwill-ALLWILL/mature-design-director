# Sound, Music, and Voice Capability

Use this capability for sonic identity, music, sound effects, narration direction, interactive audio, and audio-led experiences. Audio generators, DAWs, transcription tools, TTS providers, game engines, and codecs remain execution mechanics.

## Capability boundary

This capability owns:

- the role of sound in the user experience;
- sonic identity, arrangement, pacing, voice direction, and silence;
- continuity across music, speech, effects, and interaction;
- rights, consent, provenance, and listening evidence.

It does not own factual script content, musical rights clearance by assumption, voice consent, DAW internals, or codec implementation. A generated waveform or technically valid file is not a directed sonic experience.

## Listening contract

Define:

- listener, environment, device, attention state, and whether sound is optional;
- primary job: explain, orient, reward, warn, build atmosphere, brand, or support rhythm;
- emotional register, tempo/energy arc, density, dynamic range, and use of silence;
- relationship among voice, music, effects, UI sounds, and existing production audio;
- duration, loop, stems, sync points, loudness target, channels, and delivery formats;
- source/identity/consent and commercial-use constraints.

Do not add music or narration merely because the medium supports it. Muted, low-volume, noisy-room, headphones, and accessibility contexts may require visual/text alternatives.

## Source and rights

For every sourced or generated element, retain creator/provider, model or library where relevant, source URL, license/entitlement, retrieval or generation date, consent scope, and derivative restrictions. Do not clone a real person's voice without verified consent. Do not infer commercial rights from a downloadable file or free tier.

Generated music and effects are creative source material, not proof of ownership clearance. Keep questionable output private until rights are resolved.

## Production loop

1. Write a timed listening map with the job of each sonic event.
2. Prove the most identity-sensitive segment: opening, voice passage, transition, loop seam, or interactive response.
3. Compare credible sources/voices against the same short brief.
4. Select one sonic direction and one timing owner.
5. Produce/edit with separated stems when future balance or localization matters.
6. Listen in context, then on representative devices and low-volume playback.
7. Inspect waveform/spectrogram/loudness only as technical evidence; listening remains mandatory.
8. Fix the source or arrangement, re-export, and re-listen across boundaries.

## Voice and narration

- Direct intention, pace, emphasis, pronunciation, pauses, and relationship to visuals.
- Prefer one continuous master for identity-critical long narration; segment only with a continuity gate.
- Compare opening, middle, and closing for speaker drift, room/tone drift, prosody, clipping, repetition, and implausible duration.
- Any regenerated voice invalidates prior audiovisual approval.
- Captions/transcripts must preserve meaning, timing, names, numerals, and uncertainty.

## Music, effects, and interactive audio

- Music supports the experience arc; it must not flatten every scene into one energy level.
- Effects need semantic families, hierarchy, variation, and headroom. More layers are not automatically richer.
- UI/game sounds require idle, success, warning, error, unavailable, repeated-action, and mute behavior appropriate to the product.
- Loops must hide seams without erasing phrasing; one-shots need useful attacks and tails.
- Interactive audio must remain responsive and stable under rapid input and interruption.

## Technical and perceptual QA

Check as applicable:

- sample rate, bit depth, channel layout, codec/container, duration, sync, clipping, DC offset, silence, loop seam, and full decode;
- integrated/short-term loudness, true peak, intelligibility, masking, stereo/mono compatibility, and device translation;
- voice identity, pronunciation, emotional fit, fatigue, repetition, and boundary continuity;
- muted/subtitle alternative, reduced sensory load, user volume control, and permission/autoplay behavior.

## Evidence of completion

Sound is complete only when the actual delivered file or integrated build has been listened to end to end, its most fragile boundaries were separately reviewed, technical checks pass, rights/consent provenance is recorded, and approval/publication state is reported for the exact mix.
